# Stage 7 -- Roads (`PCG_3_1_2_Roads`), the footprint half

Contract from `docs/pypcg/stages/s07-brief.md`. Official graph: 210 nodes (38 parameter, 7 input,
1 carrier, 12 generation, 16 dropped). 136 are counted as "python" by `Tools/pypcg/port_brief.py`,
but that count treats each primitive subgraph as one node: the real computation is those 136 plus
`Intersection_solving` (170 nodes), `Crosswalks` (75) and `Custom_Projector_with_rotation_fix` (34)
reached through them. Ruling: the port surface is the whole computation half, so those primitives'
algorithms are part of this stage.

## 2. The two outputs are tagged pass-throughs, not computations

Both published pins are `PCGAddTagSettings` sitting on the output of an engine node:

- `AddTags_1 "Add Tags"` -> `RoadFootprints`, fed by `Subgraph_89 Union All Road Outlines (Road Footprint)`, a `Compose_Areas_Union`. Its content is a **union of outlines**, i.e. a computation the port owns.
- `AddTags_0 "Add Tags"` -> `ModifiedTerrain`, fed by `NamedRerouteUsage_5 "Mesh Terrain Final"` -> `NamedRerouteDeclaration_1` -> `Subgraph_37 Write Terrain (Intersection Flatten)`, a `Write_MeshTerrain`. **That one is a stateful terrain write the contract keeps native.**

Measured on the live demo level (2026-10-16): the stage-7 volume's tagged output is exactly two
items -- `PCGPolygon2DData` tagged `RoadFootprints` (842 714.39 m2, one polygon) and
`PCGMeshPartitionData` tagged `ModifiedTerrain`.

## 3. The footprint branch, as numbered steps

Each step names the official nodes it replaces.

1. **Read the Districts actor** (`Subgraph_0 Get_ActorDataByRef "Districts PCG"`). Its generated
   output is the whole stage-6 tag set: 79 items, of which 57 `localRoads`, 6 `CollectorRoads`,
   4 `ArteryRoads` (measured 2026-10-16).
2. **Split into the three classes** (`FilterDataByTag_3/4/5 "Filter ... Sections (Stripe Pass)"`).
   Each filters with `Operator = Substring`, so the classes are **substring** matches on the tag.
   Two consequences the graph relies on:
   - the closed City outline is tagged `ArteryRoads` (stage 6 publishes the boundary under that tag)
     and reaches the artery pin;
   - the six collector-tagged items are the splitter lines.
3. **Extend each open line by half its class width** (`Intersection_solving`'s
   `Subgraph_1/2/3 Extend ... Road Splines`, each `Transform_Splines_Extend` with
   `distance = <class width>/2`). A closed ring is not extended: `Transform_Splines_Extend`'s
   `InputsProcessing` graph is `InputsProcessing_ForceSpline` and the extension is along the end
   tangents, which a ring does not have.
4. **Outline each line into a ribbon** (`Subgraph_86/87/88 Transform_Outline`, `Width` = that class's
   width). See section 4 for what the CoreProcess actually computes, including the `ForceClosed`
   branch a closed ring takes.
5. **Outline the Landmarks ribbon into the local class** (`Subgraph_86`'s second `PrimaryInput` edge).
   `Subgraph_24 Transform_Areas_Expand(Expand = 800)` over `Subgraph_12` (MainTower) and `Subgraph_1`
   (CentralParkFootprint), then `GetSplineControlPoints_0` -> `CreateSpline_0` (`bClosedLoop`) gives
   the "Landmarks" spline, which is outlined at `local_Width` (`GetGraphParameter_46`).
6. **Union the ribbons** (`Subgraph_89 Compose_Areas_Union` -> `Compose_Areas_Union_CoreProcess`,
   which filters to `Polygon2D` and runs `Polygon2DOperation(Union)`).
7. **Tag and embed the widths** (`AddTags_1` writes `RoadFootprints`; `AddAttribute_2/1/0` write
   `LocalWidth` = `local_Width`, `CollectorWidth` = `collector_width`, `ArteryWidth` = `Artery_width`).

Steps 3 and 5 are what a first reading of the node list misses, and together they are worth 25 % of
the footprint's area: taking the Districts output as open lines only gives -25.5 %, and each of the
two recovers most of that (section 6.1).

Because step 2 reads the **Districts** output rather than the solved sections, the footprint is the
outline union over the districts' own road lines. The naive check agrees: the open lines' lengths
times their widths give 671 302 m2, and the union loses 6.5 % of that to overlaps, which is the
scale a connected road grid implies.

## 4. `Transform_Outline` -- the one thing that had to be measured

The CoreProcess (`Transform_Outline_CoreProcess`, 13 nodes) computes:

```
FilterDataByAttribute_0  keep $IsClosed == false
CreatePolygon2D_2        ForceClosed          <- that filtered output
OffsetPolygon2D_0 / _1   +- width/2          <- CreatePolygon2D_2
Polygon2DOperation_0     Difference           <- grown minus shrunk
CreatePolygon2D_0        ForceOpen, OpenPolygonWidth = width, EndType = Butt
output node              wires BOTH CreatePolygon2D_0 and Polygon2DOperation_0
```

The published output is the **join of two branches**: the `Polygon2DOperation` difference *and* the
raw `CreatePolygon2D_0` band. Which one carries the content depends on the input:

- For an **open** path, `CreatePolygon2D_0`'s `ForceOpen` band is the ribbon, and it is the branch
  that survives. `CreatePolygon2D_2`'s `ForceClosed` ring of an open path is degenerate, both offsets
  of it are empty, and `Polygon2DOperation_0` outputs nothing.
- For a **closed** path, `CreatePolygon2D_0`'s band is the annulus around the ring and
  `Polygon2DOperation_0` is empty for the same reason.

**Measured, 2026-10-16** (`pyg_probe_t10_cal.py`): a 36 118.85 cm straight 2-point path at width 1900
gives a band of exactly `36118.85 x 1900` cm = 6862.58 m2, spanning `+-950` and capped square at both
ends. So `ForceOpen(band)` with `EndType = Butt` is `UE::Geometry::PolygonsOffset` on an open
poly-path (`PCGCreatePolygon2D.cpp:243-269`; `Butt` = "offsets both sides of a path, with square
blunt ends", `PCGPolygon2DUtils.h:24`), and **the ribbon is `width` wide, once**. An earlier reading
that added a `+-width/2` follow-up doubled every ribbon; the engine call makes that unnecessary and
the measurement rejects it.

Wired to the engine as `pypcg.ue.ops.Ops.path_offset` (GeometryScript
`CreatePolygonsFromPathOffset`), which is literally the same `PolygonsOffset` call the PCG node makes.

## 5. Deliberate differences and what is not reproduced yet

1. **`SimpleRoads` is not branched on in the footprint half.** The official graph branches six times
   on `simpleRoads`, but the footprint's `Stripe Pass` filters are fed from `NamedRerouteUsage_2`
   (Districts) directly, ahead of every `simpleRoads` branch, so the footprint does not depend on it.
   The parameter is kept so the slim graph carries it for the kept grammar nodes, and
   `test_simple_roads_produces_one_class_only` pins that changing it does not change the footprint.
2. **The landmark ring's vertex set is passed through whole.** `GetSplineControlPoints_0` ->
   `CreateSpline_0(bClosedLoop)` rebuilds the Landmarks spline from the expanded ring's vertices, and
   the port uses the ring it computed rather than re-reading the engine node's own vertex list. This
   is one of the open candidates for the remaining -0.65 % (section 7). Note that
   `Extract_Segments` is **not** a difference here: it is on the sections branch
   (`Subgraph_3` -> `Gather_0` -> `Subgraph_2`), not the footprint's.
3. **The `$*` attributes** the footprint carries: the official pipeline writes `LocalWidth`,
   `CollectorWidth`, `ArteryWidth` through `PCGAddAttributeSettings`, whose `OutputTarget` is
   `@data.LocalWidth` etc. -- per-*data* attributes on the Polygon2D, not per-vertex. The slim graph
   keeps the three `AddAttribute_*` nodes, so the attributes come from the engine nodes, not Python.
4. **`Crosswalks` and the two `Write_MeshTerrain` stay native** by contract (Ruling 2), so the 75-node
   Crosswalks primitive and the terrain writes are not ported. The `Crosswalks` node reads
   `Crosswalk_distance_from_intersection = 250` (its own override) and the three road widths.
5. **The terrain half is not ported** (commit 2; section 8). `ModifiedTerrain` is therefore absent
   from the slim graph's output rather than empty, which is what V2 reports as "tag missing".

**A real finding from the same run, not about this port.** V2's determinism check failed with *"the
official graph did not reproduce its own output after restore"*: two official generations of stage 7
gave **286 232** and **286 371** instances (0.05 %), with three meshes shifting
(`SM_Lane_Car_200_200_A` 104 584 -> 104 602, `_B` 104 555 -> 104 691, `SM_Lane_Divider_40_201`
19 832 -> 19 817). The official graph is therefore **not bit-reproducible run to run** on this
machine. That is why `official_after` cannot serve as a determinism reference for this stage, and it
is worth knowing for every stage whose determinism check leans on it.

**A known limit of this stage's verification, recorded rather than hidden.** `ModifiedTerrain` is a
`PCGMeshPartitionData` and `pypcg.v2.describe` gives it `kind: "other"` with zero area, zero length
and zero points. V2's check on that tag therefore reduces to **"the tag is present"**: the count,
area, length and points metrics are all skipped because the official value is 0. So V2 does *not*
verify the terrain flattening at all, and a slim run that emitted an empty surface would pass as long
as the tag was there. `RoadFootprints` is the only output this stage's V2 meaningfully checks (one
polygon, so ruling N10's "fewer than 5 items" skip does not apply to count, but an area comparison
over a single item is still a weak instrument).

## 7. Open, with the evidence

The footprint is **-0.65 %** from the official area offline and **-1.61 %** in the V2 run, with
`maxX` 109 013.9 against the official 109 548.2 (534 cm short). What the two construction facts are
worth is measured, and they are:

- the closed City outline is part of the footprint. `FilterDataByTag_5`'s substring match puts it on
  the artery pin, and `Transform_Outline`'s CoreProcess outlines a closed ring on its `ForceClosed`
  branch (`CreatePolygon2D_2` -> `OffsetPolygon2D(+-width/2)` -> Difference). Including it takes the
  union from -25.5 % to -4.25 % and is what makes three of the four official bbox edges land exactly;
- the Landmarks ribbon belongs to the local class. `Subgraph_86` is the only outline node with a
  second `PrimaryInput` edge, its `Width` is `local_Width`, and the expanded CentralPark ring reaches
  x = 108 228 -- further than any road line. Including it takes the union from -4.25 % to -0.65 % and
  moves `maxX` from 105 349.8 to 109 013.9.

**The cause of the remaining 534 cm and 0.65 % is NOT established, and `Extract_Segments` cannot be
it.** Two reasons, both checkable against the wiring:

- `Subgraph_3 Extract Road Segments` (`CutAngleThreshold = 3`) reads the Landmarks declaration and
  feeds `Gather_0` -> `Subgraph_2 Intersection_solving`, i.e. the **sections**. The footprint's
  landmark ribbon comes from `Subgraph_24` -> `FilterDataByType_5` -> `GetSplineControlPoints_0` ->
  `CreateSpline_0` -> `NamedRerouteDeclaration_2`, and its only outline consumer is `Subgraph_86`.
  A different branch of the same declaration;
- `CutAngleThreshold` makes `Extract_Segments` split a path at bends, which **removes** vertices. It
  cannot move a maximum outward, and the official is *wider* than the port, not narrower.

The arithmetic also rules out the obvious version of the landmark story: the expanded ring's own
`maxX` is 108 228.43, plus the ribbon's 950 half-width gives 109 178.4, while the port measures
109 013.9 and the official is 109 548.2 -- so **the official is about 370 cm beyond what this
landmark ribbon can reach**, and the landmark alone cannot explain the gap in either direction.

Untested candidates, in the order worth testing:

1. **The landmark ring's vertex set.** `GetSplineControlPoints_0` reads `PolyLineData::GetNumVertices`
  and `CreateSpline_0` rebuilds with `bClosedLoop`; the port passes the expanded ring's own vertices
  straight through. If `Transform_Areas_Expand`'s
  `InputsProcessing_ConvertClosedSplinesAndForwardPoly2Ds` delivers a different vertex count (the
  measurement above used `path_of`, which prefers a dense polyline) the ribbon is a different shape.
2. **The ring's start vertex**, which sets where the ribbon's offset begins. A ring's first vertex
  decides the Butt cap's placement for a band that is nearly closed on itself.
3. **The ribbon width applied to the landmark.** This port uses `local_Width` (1900) because
  `Subgraph_86` is the local outline node. Nothing in the graph contradicts it, but the landmark
  ribbon's contribution to `maxX` has not been measured on its own.

Nothing in this section is a threshold change: the footprint's area check is a V2 metric against the
official value, and the gap is a port question, not a randomness question, so it must be fixed in the
port rather than absorbed by `v2_thresholds.json`.

## 8. Commit 2, the terrain half: blocked, with the cause

The terrain half (`ModifiedTerrain`) is **not done**. Its shape is known from the graph:

```
Subgraph_45 Extract_MeshTerrainPoints (Road Zone) <- GetMeshTerrain_RoadProjection
Subgraph_44 Transform_Project   <- Subgraph_45 (points) + AttributeMathsOp_7 (the height source)
BlurElement_0 "Blur Terrain (Road Edges)" <- FilterDataByType_3 <- Subgraph_49
Subgraph_49 Place_Point_AtCenter <- Subgraph_42 Transform_Level(Average) <- Subgraph_31
Subgraph_31 Transform_Project   <- IntersectionsPlanar + GetMeshTerrain_RoadProjection
Subgraph_46 Write_MeshTerrain (Road Flatten)                       [KEPT, native]
  -> Reroute_7 -> Subgraph_33 Extract_MeshTerrainPoints (Intersection Zone)
               -> Subgraph_53 Transform_Resample(Distance 500) -> Subgraph_50 Transform_Project
Subgraph_36 Transform_Project   <- Subgraph_33 + Subgraph_50
BlurElement_2 "Blur Terrain (Final Pass)" -> FilterDataByType_6
Subgraph_37 Write_MeshTerrain (Intersection Flatten)               [KEPT, native]
```

**One thing that looked like a blocker is not one: a `PCGMeshPartitionData` collapses to points.**
Measured 2026-10-16 (`pyg_probe_t10_mesh2.py`): the stage-7 volume's `ModifiedTerrain` surface gives
`to_point_data()` -> `PCGPointData` with **263 358** points, first at (112500, 112500, 585.235), and
the project's own `pypcg.ue.convert.points_of` already reads it (same count, same first point). So
`Extract_MeshTerrainPoints`' *output* -- what `Subgraph_44` and `Subgraph_36` consume -- is
reachable from Python, and Ruling 3's plan (two Python nodes chained around the two kept
`Write_MeshTerrain` nodes via `more_modules`) is feasible in principle.

**The real blocker is `Subgraph_31`.** Its `PrimaryInput` is `NamedRerouteUsage_17 "Intersections
Planar"` <- `NamedRerouteDeclaration_6` <- **`Subgraph_2 Intersection_solving`'s
`IntersectionsPlanar` output**. So the intersection-zone flatten cannot be computed without the
170-node `Intersection_solving` primitive, and that primitive is in turn the source of:
`ArterySections` / `CollectorSections` / `LocalSections` (which every kept grammar node and the whole
decal/crosswalk chain reads), and `RoadsFootprints` (the primitive's own internal one, distinct from
the stage's published `RoadFootprints` the footprint half computes).

So commit 2 is `Intersection_solving` (170 nodes: `Loop_0` over the intersection expansion, 11
`Transform_Outline` calls, 12 `Compose_Spline_Cut`, 6 `Transform_Offset`, 3
`Transform_Splines_Extend`, 3 `Filter_Splines_byLength`, 2 `Compose_Areas_Union` for the clip cutters,
1 `Compose_Areas_Exclusion` per class) plus five more PCGPrimitives (`Transform_Project`,
`Transform_Level`, `Place_Point_AtCenter`, `PCGBlurSettings`, `Transform_Resample`). That is the
majority of the stage, not a remainder, and it is why this port is split.

**Nothing about it is blocked in principle.** Every input it needs is reachable: the providers come
in on pins, the mesh partition enumerates, and the two `Write_MeshTerrain` nodes stay native. What is
missing is the volume of work, not a capability.
