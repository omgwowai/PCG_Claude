# Stage 1 -- Terrain (`PCG_1_1_Terrain`, 地形底座)

The island the city stands on. The official graph extracts the base mesh terrain's vertices as points, raises
hills on them with spatial noise, pulls everything outside a circle (`Terrain_shaping_1`) down to a flat sea
floor with a 500 m falloff, flattens the city footprint with a 100 m falloff, blends the two by `CityFlatness`
and writes the result back as a mesh-terrain modifier at sub-priority 1. Every later stage stands on it: stage 7
reads it as `Intermediate` at sub-priority 2, stages 8, 10, 11, 13, 14, 15, 16, 17, 18 read the `Final` terrain
(section 7).

Numbers below are from the official graph run as a TAP graph on a spawned probe actor with the stage-1 volume's
transform (`(0, 0, 0)`, scale `(25, 25, 10)`, component seed 42, the demo's `CityFlatness` 0.6000000238418579,
`HillsHeight` 7001), 2026-10-20; the stage-1 volume itself was not touched. Distilled into
`Tools/pypcg/data/s01_fixture.json` by `Tools/pypcg/s01_fixture.py`; the native-node measurements into
`Tools/pypcg/data/s01_native_measurements.json` by `Tools/pypcg/s01_measure_offline.py`.

## 0. Step A+ -- the point count, measured before porting

| | |
|---|---|
| 2 km box (`Get_MeshTerrain` `MeshPartitionBoundSize` 200000, stage 1's own node otherwise) | **116 281** points, a 341 x 341 grid, spacing **585.9375 cm** |
| Area of that box (point extremes) | 3 968 811 m2 -> **0.029299 points per m2** |
| `convert.from_pcg` (PCG -> `geo.Points`, 2 attributes) | 2.175 s = **18.7 us per point** |
| `convert.to_pcg` (the point writer, 2 attributes) | 2.744 s = **23.6 us per point** (trap 57 measured 11.3 us without attributes) |
| Extrapolated to the stage's 3 km query | 0.029299 x 3000^2 m2 = **263 689** points |
| Measured: the stage's own extraction (tap t01) | **261 312** points, bbox +-149 414.0625 cm, every z -600 (the base terrain is flat) |

**261 312 is under the ~300 000 line**, so the per-point parts were candidates for Python. Step F then decided
them one by one, on timings, not estimates (`s01_native_measurements.json`):

| Official node | Per-point work | Measured in Python | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| `SpatialNoise_Hills` (`Transform_Points_SpatialNoise`) | its inner `BlurElement_1` averages every point over a 12 000 cm sphere, twice | **852 us per point** (1 261 neighbours each) -> **445 s** for the two iterations; and its sum order is the octree's, not reproducible | **native** |
| `FlattenProject_AroundCircle`, `Subgraph_5` (`Transform_Project`) | `CustomHLSL_1` (GPU): the 2D distance to the nearest of the target polygon's surface samples (0.5 per m2) | the nearest-target search alone, in doubles: **138 us per point -> 36 s per projection, 72 s for the two**, before the float32 emulation and the conversions | **native** |
| `AttributeMathsOp_0` (Lerp) | `A + (B - A) * Ratio` per position component | reproduces **783 936 of 783 936** official components bit for bit; costs only the conversions, 2 x 18.7 + 23.6 us = **~16 s** | **Python** |

The projection is also not reproducible bit for bit in Python. With an exact emulation of the primitive -- the
sampler grid (cell `sqrt(2e4)` cm, offset `(cell - 0.002) / 2`, ratio 1), the 2D float32 distance, the remap
in double into a float, `FRichCurve`'s float Bezier for the cubic key, the double Lerp -- **247 241 of 261 312**
flattened heights come out bit for bit; the other 14 071 differ by **one float32 ulp of the GPU's distance**
(11 424 at -1 ulp, 2 642 at +1, 5 at -2), at most 0.0012 cm in z. That is the GPU's `sqrt`/`dot`, not the
port's arithmetic (no other evaluation order matched them all), and trap 62 says downstream stages see last
bits. So the projection stays on the GPU it was measured on.

## 2. The computation

### 2.1 `s01_terrain` (the masks)

1. **Footprint** -- `Subgraph_1 Extract_Bounds` + `TraceBoundFootprint_TerrainShaping1 Trace_Bound_Footprint`
   (`PlanarSurface` **Top**, `ScaleFactor` 1): every extracted point's own box in one FBox (the same helper as
   stage 18's, now `geo.ground.footprint`), its rectangle at the box's TOP (`ResetPointCenter` z ratio 1).
   Official: +-149 415.0625 cm at z -599 (the points sit at -600 with bounds +-1).
2. **Expand** -- `Subgraph_2 Transform_Areas_Expand` 60 000 cm: +-209 415.0625 cm.
3. **Down** -- `Subgraph_0 Transform_Offset` (`PerPoints` false) z -1820.424606: the square at z -2419.424606.
4. **The circle down** -- `Subgraph_6 Transform_Offset` the same on `Terrain_shaping_1`; only its outline is
   used next, so the z does not matter for the result.
5. **Square minus circle** -- `ComposeExclusion_SquareMinusCircle Compose_Areas_Exclusion`: one polygon, the
   square with the circle as its hole (official: 4 + 1185 vertices, 12 468 329 m2), at z -2419.424606. ->
   `FlattenArea`.
6. **The city flat zone** -- `AttributeMathsOp_2` sets the City outline's control points' z to 0, `Subgraph_4
   Transform_Offset` lifts it 3000 cm: the same closed curve at z 3000 (tangents unchanged). -> `CityFlatZone`.

### 2.2 Native, between the two Python nodes

    ExtractMeshTerrainPoints_0 -> SpatialNoise_Hills (NoiseAmplitude = HillsHeight; distort 33000 cm x3, caustic
      noise x2, blur 12000 cm x2) -> FlattenProject_AroundCircle (onto FlattenArea, falloff 0..50000 cm)
      -> Subgraph_5 (onto CityFlatZone as a polygon, falloff 0..10000 cm)

### 2.3 `s01_terrain_blend` (the city-flattening decision)

7. **Ratio** -- `AttributeMathsOp_1 OneMinus`: `1 - CityFlatness` in double (0.3999999761581421 on the demo).
8. **Blend** -- `AttributeMathsOp_0 Lerp($Position of FlattenProject, $Position of Subgraph_5, Ratio)`: each
   component `A + (B - A) * Ratio`; every other field and attribute (`SourcePositions`, which the writer uses to
   find each vertex) from A. -> `Terrain` -> `WriteMeshTerrain_0`.

## 3. Deliberate differences

- The polygons are Python's (`MakePolygon2DData`, frame from their own vertices) and the city flat zone is a
  world-space spline with the identity transform, where the official one keeps the City actor's (10, 10, 0)
  translation. The native projections read them only through which surface-sampler grid nodes fall inside and
  through the plane's z; a vertex moved by 1e-11 .. 6e-3 cm flips a grid node only if one lies that close to the
  outline (expected ~4e-4 nodes on the city's 6 km outline). Section 6 measures the whole result.
- The stage draws no random number: `RANDOM = False`. The native noise draws from its own seed, as officially.

## 4. What could not be reproduced, and what was done instead

- The blur and the GPU projection: kept native (section 0). The hills noise is the brief's "Python" item; it is
  not Python here.

## 5. Corrections to the brief

1. **"Inside the City the height spread is under 500 cm"** is false for the official graph: **4 254 cm** (276 ..
   4 530 cm over the 68 752 points inside the City). The Lerp keeps `CityFlatness` of the hills:
   `final = hills + (flat - hills) * (1 - CityFlatness)`, so the spread is about `CityFlatness` x the hills'
   (0.6 x 7 090). Replaced by what the graph guarantees: inside the City every height equals that blend of the
   two native surfaces (bit for bit), the flat surface lies within 2 cm of the city plane (2998.70 .. 3000.70),
   and the mean is `CityFlatness * mean(hills) + (1 - CityFlatness) * mean(flat)` (2482.657 cm).
2. **`CityFlatness` works the other way round from its tooltip** ("0 = fully hilly, 1 = fully flat"): the
   ratio is `1 - CityFlatness`, so 1 gives the hills unchanged and 0 the flat city. Kept as official.
3. **"The outer ring differs from the core by more than 1000 cm"** holds: core (r < 500 m) mean 2 836.9 cm,
   outer ring (r > 1 400 m) mean -2 162.7 cm.
4. **"The written point count equals the extracted count"** holds: 261 312.
5. **"The same seed writes the same heights twice"**: no Python randomness; the check is that two slim runs
   write identical heights.
6. **The exclusion is square minus circle** (the official title "Square Minus Circle"), not circle minus square.
7. **"Python: hills noise"**: native (section 0).
8. **`stages.DOWNSTREAM[1] = [7, 10, 18]` is incomplete**: every stage with a `Get_MeshTerrain` reads stage 1
   (section 7).
