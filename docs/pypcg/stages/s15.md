## 5. The rotation chain's pivot (the one non-obvious rule)

Every spline the ring/arm chain makes carries **the same data frame translation**:
`(−613.8667464186474, 142.0539320733078, 2800.0000000000146)`, rotation identity. `Transform_Offset`
with `PerPoints` false inverts that frame, applies the yaw to the control points **about the frame's
origin**, and transforms back:

    world' = origin + q_yaw (p − origin)

Rotating any piece of any arm by ±120° (and the base by −60°) about that origin reproduces the
official output to **1e-12 cm on every vertex**; no other pivot candidate (the piece's centroid, its box
centre, the world origin) does. The Z of the origin is irrelevant to a yaw. Both helper and its
fixture test are in `pypcg.geo.frames.rotate_in_frame` / `test_s15_helpers.py`.

The `Transform_Align` rule is equally simple and equally measured: **the whole input set's world box
centre moves to the second input's box centre** (X and Y only; Z untouched because `Justify_Z` is None).
It is a SET operation — `CombinePoints` over every input item — not a per-item shift (`geo.frames.align`).
Consequence (Task 22): since every turned set is aligned afterwards, the pivot only matters for the
intermediates; the sectors are the same for any pivot (1.4e-12 cm), and the port turns about the origin.

## 8. What is not reproduced (blockers for a continuation)

1. **`Subgraph_2` `Create_Spline_Shape`'s copy onto the six outline-centre points (`CopyPoints_0` +
   `CreateConstant_0` Width 2500). — SOLVED 2026-10-20, `geo.frames.copy_shape`, pinned by
   `test_s15_helpers.py`.** Each copy is a rigid turn about the copy's own centroid:
   - the **hexagon** (`Subgraph_2`, Ngon 6, Width = Length 2500, so its radius is `Width / 2` = 1250) is
     stage 4's 6-gon in its `ENGINE_DEG` order turned by **`60° − yaw`**; all 36 tapped vertices
     reproduce to **1.6e-12 cm**;
   - the **square** (`Subgraph_9`, 700, Ngon 4) is the `(−350,−350) (−350,350) (350,350) (350,−350)`
     ring turned by **`yaw − 90°`**; all 24 tapped vertices reproduce to **0 cm**.

   `yaw` is the `Place_Point_AtCenter` point's own `$Rotation`. **The copy's CENTRE is not that point:**
   the squares' centroids ARE the six points, but the hexagons' are the six vertices of the inner 32.5 m
   ring (`circumradius 4247.2233`, each within 1.9e-12 cm of a tapped inner-ring vertex). A port that
   puts the hexagons on the points is 649.02 cm off, each.
   Caveat stated rather than hidden: a regular hexagon is invariant under a 60° turn with an index shift,
   and all six yaws here are multiples of 60°, so the hexagon's *sign* of yaw cannot be separated from
   that shift; `60 − yaw` is the form that reproduces the tapped vertex ORDER. The square, which repeats
   only every 90°, fixes `yaw − 90°` with no ambiguity.
2. **`Subgraph_10`'s union order and `Subgraph_11`'s ring start.** The union's area and the expand's
   area are measured (4338.0 → one piece; 6 pieces of 710.9 m²), but the ring's FIRST vertex (trap 64)
   is not checked against the kept `Subgraph_12/13` grammar nodes, which are where it would matter.
3. **`PlaceTreesOnSpline`'s sampler. — SOLVED 2026-10-20 for geometry and yaw,
   `geo.frames.sample_on_spline`, pinned by `test_s15_helpers.py`.** `N = int(L / spacing)` = 44 on the
   44 535.898 cm tree ring, `L / N` = 1012.1795 cm apart; the first sample sits ON the ring's **LAST**
   control point and the walk runs **backwards** through the control points (last → first → …); a sample
