Python: `Content/Python/pypcg/stages/s14_parkings.py` (everything before the two kept grammar nodes) and
`s14_parkings_finish.py` (everything after them); shared helpers in `pypcg/geo/splines.py` (new: the open-path
primitives) and `pypcg/geo/areas.py` (`follow_subjects`, new); graph: `pypcg/graphs/s14_parkings.py`; checks:
`pypcg/checks/s14.py`; tests: `Tools/pypcg/tests/test_s14_parkings.py`. Measured on the demo level, 2026-10-20,
against the official graph at component seed 42 on the official stage-13 upstream (5 lots): a tap graph of the
official nodes (`Content/Python/pypcg_s14_measure.py`, 66 taps), distilled in
`Tools/pypcg/data/s14_official_measurements.json`.

## 2. The computation

Read from the official IR (`Tools/pypcg/official_ir/PCG_3_2_3_Parkings.json`) and the primitives' own graphs
(exported 2026-10-20 with `PyPCGToolsLibrary.export_graph_ir`, `pyg_probe_t19_export.py`), and checked step by step
against the taps (section 6).

### 2.1 Areas and lines (`s14_parkings`)

1. **Outer area** -- `Subgraph_29 Transform_Areas_Contract` (300 cm; `UnionBeforeOutputting` true by default):
   `geo.areas.contract`, then `geo.areas.follow_subjects` (2.3).
2. **Inner area** -- `Subgraph_33` (800 cm, union) on the outer areas. The 20 x 25 m lot has none.
3. **Cells** -- `Subgraph_32 Subdivide_Areas_withGrid`, size mode, `GridWidth` 1900 / `GridLength` 4500: the lines of
   `geo.grid_lines` (the frame of the ring's FIRST longest edge) cut the area (`ops.cut_with_paths`, stage 9's
   reading). 52 cells.
4. **Aisles** -- `Subgraph_30` (500 cm, union) on the cells: 36 (the 9 x 35 m middles of the 19 x 45 m cells).
5. **Central lines** -- `Subgraph_35`, subdivision mode (1, 2): `geo.grid_lines`' one Y line per aisle, REVERSED --
   the official lines run the other way (36 of 36). -> `CentralLines` (and the barrier grammar).
6. **Rows** -- `Subgraph_45 Transform_Resample` (`Distance` 350, `FitToCurve`): `geo.splines.resample_fit`, the
   engine's `FDistanceStepSampler` -- `N = int(L / 350)` samples `L / (N - 1)` apart, and NO spline when `N < 2`
   (no sample under 350 cm, and a one-sample spline does not survive `CreateSpline`: of the 36 central lines the one
   of 339 cm is dropped, 35 rows; of the 38 cut outer pieces the 3 under 350 cm and the 4 under 700 cm, 31 lines);
   then `Subgraph_46 / 47 Transform_Offset` (0, +-300, 0): each control point moved along its own left normal
   (`TransformPoints_1` -> `OffsetSpline_0` turns the offset by the control point's rotation, the line's direction).
7. **Medians** -- `Subgraph_56 Transform_Areas_Expand` (200 cm; no union, the primitive's default) on every lot, then
   `Subgraph_55` subdivision mode (2, 2): the X and the Y middle line of each, reversed as in 5. 10 lines.
8. **Median bands** -- `Subgraph_57 Transform_Outline` (800): `geo.roads.outline` (the FULL width to
   `path_offset`, Butt ends). They cut the outer lines and the border: the entrances.
9. **Outer lines** -- `Subgraph_36 Extract_Segments` (2 deg) on the outer areas (`geo.extract_segments`, 21 runs) ->
   `Subgraph_43 Transform_Truncate` (500 / 500): `geo.splines.truncate` -- `SplitSpline(ByDistance)` keeps what is
   after the start offset, `ReverseSpline`, the same at the end offset, and NEVER reversed back: the output runs
   from the old end (every tap); a run not longer than an offset keeps that side (978 cm -> 478 cm, measured) ->
   `Subgraph_44 Transform_Resample (NumberOfSamples)`: the run's chord, its two ends (21 of 21, 2 to 12 points in,
   2 out) -> `Subgraph_61 Compose_Spline_Cut` (Difference) by the median bands (`geo.sections.cut_paths`: 38
   pieces) -> `Subgraph_62` (350, fitted): 31 lines (the 7 pieces under 700 cm vanish).
10. **Border** -- `Subgraph_59 Extract_Segments` (2 deg) on every lot -> `Subgraph_58 Transform_Resample`
    (Subdivision 5: every segment in six, `geo.splines.subdivide`) -> `Subgraph_60 Compose_Spline_Cut` by the
    median bands: 41 runs. -> `Border` (both grammars).
11. **Arrow lines** -- `Subgraph_63 Transform_Truncate` (600 / 600) on the medians (so reversed), then
    `Subgraph_66` (-100, +200) and `Subgraph_67` (+100, -200), the second reversed (`Subgraph_68
    Transform_Splines_Reverse`).

Every spline handed to a kept node carries its control points' rotations (`geo.Spline.rotations`, the direction of
travel, trap 63): what `GetSplineControlPoints` gives the official ones.

### 2.2 Points (`s14_parkings`)

1. **Spaces** -- `Subgraph_38 Extract_Segments` with `CutAngleThreshold` 0 (its default: the predicate becomes
   `$Index + 1`, so the spline splits at EVERY control point) on row A, row B, the outer lines (its input order) ->
   `Subgraph_40 Place_Point_AtCenter` (`ForcePolygonKeepPoints` makes each 2-point run a polygon; the frame of its
   first longest segment; the bounding-box centre; extents 100; roll 0): one point per space, `geo.splines.center`.
2. **Line decals** -- `Subgraph_52 Place_OnSpline` (`UseExistingControlPoints`, `Facing` tangent) on the same lines:
   every control point, turned along the line, box +-1, steepness 1 -> `Subgraph_7 Assign_Assets`
   (`SM_Yellow_Line_Parking_inst`, `PositionOffset` z 3, `ExtentsScale` (1.2, 1, 1), `UseMeshBounds` false, uniform
   scale false). -> `Lines`.
3. **Arrows** -- `Subgraph_65 Place_OnSpline` (`UseExistingControlPoints`, `Facing` inward = the default: yaw +90,
   `TransformPoints_1`) on arrow side A, then side B -> `Subgraph_8 Assign_Assets` (`SM_mesh_decal_arrow_head`,
   `PositionOffset` z 40, everything else default). -> `Arrows`.
4. **Cars** -- `Subgraph_48 Filter_RandomChoice` (Ratio 0.8, `RandomPerDataset` FALSE: one Bernoulli per space,
   `geo.keep_each` on the points) then `Subgraph_49` (0.5) on the occupied ones: selected -> `Subgraph_4
   Assign_Assets` (seven vehicles, `RotationOffset` yaw +90, `RandomRotationRange` z 5), unselected -> `Subgraph_5`
   (yaw -90, `RandomPositionRange` y 50, `RandomRotationRange` z 5): `geo.assets.choose` + `expand_items` with
   `ctx.rng`. Static meshes: the output is the moved targets. -> `ForwardCars`, `ReverseCars`.
5. **Ground tiles** -- `Subgraph_10 Fill_Areas_Uniformly` with `asset` `SM_Lane_Car_200_200`: the CoreProcess takes
   the point extents from the mesh's bounds (`BoundsFromMesh_0`: (100, 100, 10)), so a 2 m grid
   (`geo.ground.fill_uniform`, stage 10's), `Asset` set on every point -> `Subgraph_11` (z -19). -> `Tiles`.

### 2.3 Where the engine's booleans put a ring's first vertex (`geo.areas.follow_subjects`)

The grid of step 2.1.3 is laid from the ring's FIRST longest edge, and the two long sides of a rectangle tie, so the
start vertex of the contract's output decides the grid's phase. Measured on the taps: every result of an offset
starts at the vertex nearest its subject's first vertex (outer 5 of 5, inner 4 of 4, median expansion 5 of 5,
aisles 33 of 36 -- the three others carry duplicated vertices), and the union hands its results back in subject
order. `follow_subjects` applies both rules; without it the computed rings started elsewhere and 8 central lines
came out on the other phase.

### 2.4 After the grammars (`s14_parkings_finish`, all ten official nodes)

1. **`Subgraph_3 Custom_Projector_with_rotation_fix`** on every item, in its In Points order (`Subgraph_7`, `53`,
   `54`, the tiles, the arrows, the forward and the reverse cars): `geo.ground.project_point` (the point's scale
   and engine quaternion place the corner probes, trap 69; a miss stays at density 0 with its z doubled).
2. **`Subgraph_9 Transform_Offset` (0, 0, 22)** -- non-absolute: `RotateVector(q, offset)` with the point's NEW,
   terrain-tilted rotation. A vertical lift put the official points up to 5.4 cm off; along the tilted up axis,
   2.4e-4 cm (6.3).
3. **`AttributeCast_1`** `AssetString`; **`FilterDataByAttribute_1`** (`SM_veh`, Substring): the vehicles;
   **`FilterDataByType_0`, `AttributeStringOp_1 / 2`** (`/Mesh/` -> `/Mesh/Transparent/`, `SM_veh` ->
   `SM_All_Trans_veh`), **`CopyAttributes_0`**, **`AttributeCast_0`**: every car's transparent twin; **`Gather_0`**:
   the vehicles, the rest, the twins. -> `Points` -> the kept `Subgraph_13`.

The grammar points (14 attributes: `Symbol` Name, `Module Mesh`, `Scalable`, `DebugColor` Vector4, ...) cross the
node through `convert` unchanged; the spawner reads `Asset`.

**Surface:** 81 official nodes, 68 computation by the brief; all 68 are in Python (23 of them named reroutes --
9 declarations, 14 usages -- that became edges, so about 45 real computation nodes), none stays native. The scene
reads and the three generation nodes are kept; `Branch_1`, the partition tail and the disabled debug node are
dropped.

## 3. Deliberate differences

1. **New random numbers** (spec E2): occupancy, reversal, the car picks and their yaw / position ranges are
   `ctx.rng`, through one `Draws` object (`keep(step, n, ratio)`), so a check can substitute the official outcome.
2. **No density noise.** The official `Place_Point_AtCenter` and `Fill_Areas_Uniformly` end in an `AttributeNoise`
   on `$Density` (spaces 0.34, 0.99, ...; tiles 0.09, 0.88, ...); nothing downstream reads density (the spawner does
   not filter on it; the projector writes 0 on a miss), so the port leaves density 1.
3. **Point seeds** are 0 (+ `dataIndex` in each `Assign_Assets`), not the engine's position-derived ones: the static
   mesh spawner does not read them, and the cars draw from `ctx.rng`.
4. **No partition, no debug** (`Branch_1`, `Subgraph_12 GridPartition`, `Loop_0`, `Subgraph_2`).

## 4. What could not be reproduced

1. **One lot's grid phase: a last-bit tie.** The inner rectangle of lot 1 has long edges of 7900.000000000015 and
   7900.000000000018 cm officially (the first longest is edge 3) and 7900.000000000003 / 7899.999999999979 in the
   port (edge 1), so the port lays that lot's 19 m grid from the other long side (the rings agree to 1.3e-8 cm;
   `inner_ring_1_edge_lengths` in the data file). Reproducing it needs the official ring bit for bit -- its
   Clipper output in the polygon's stored frame (trap 68, s10.md 4.1). On this level the lot is symmetric, so the
   totals stay equal (733 spaces, 834 line points end to end); its 8 central lines sit elsewhere.
2. **The subdivision-mode lines are a centimetre-level approximation.** From the TAPPED official aisles, the port's
   central lines are the official ones 0.41 cm to the side, the medians 0.40 cm; the official lines are not exact
   middle lines either: 3498.59 .. 3500.39 cm long against the port's 3499 .. 3500, carrying z -0.003 .. -2.5 cm
   (central) and +1 .. -3 cm (medians) and the matching pitch, where the port's are flat. Through
   `RestoreOrigZPos` that z reaches the final barrier, line-decal, car and arrow heights (<= 3 cm). It also feeds the
   barrier grammar, whose scalable modules fit each line's length from its start: **558 barriers against the official
   557 on every seed** (`Concrete_Barrier_02` -1, `Concrete_Barrier_06` +2). The fence grammar reads the border only
   and is equal per mesh. `geo.grid_lines` is stage 9's helper; its subdivision mode is left as it is.
3. **Vertex structure of the booleans.** The official cells keep a collinear vertex where a neighbouring grid line
   meets their edge, and some aisles carry duplicated vertices; the port's do not. Areas and positions are equal
   (cells to 9.6e-11 cm where the vertex counts agree), and the central lines from the official aisles all match.
4. **Fitted spacing on curve splines (<= 0.59 cm).** The cut outer lines are curve splines with chord tangents
   officially; the engine's arc-length table spaces their samples up to 0.59 cm from the exact even spacing the
   port computes on the straight line.
5. **Piece order of `Compose_Spline_Cut`.** The official clip hands its pieces back in another order (outer cut 38,
   border 41: the same sets, every piece matched by its midpoint); nothing downstream depends on the order.

## 5. Corrections to the brief

1. **"The number of spaces is within +-10 % of area / (space area)"** is not what the official graph computes:
   733 official spaces on 42 100 m2 of lot is 57 m2 a space (a 3.5 x 6 m space is 21 m2) -- the aisles, the medians,
   the outer band's corners and the inner rows' end gaps take the rest. The check asserts the exact rule instead:
   the spaces are the 350 cm segments of the space lines, `sum(int(L / 350) - 1)`.
2. **"`MeshTerrain` (Final)" as an input** of the computation: only the post-grammar projector reads it, so it is the
   second Python node's input.
3. **"68 in Python"** is met: all 68, about 45 real computation nodes (2.4).
4. **"two rows from the outer and inner lines by shortening, resampling and +-Y offsets"**: the inner rows are the
   aisles' centre lines resampled and offset +-300 cm; the outer lines are shortened (500 cm a side, reversed),
   turned into their chords, cut at the entrances and resampled -- they are one row, not two.
