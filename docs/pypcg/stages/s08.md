# Stage 8 -- Highways (`PCG_3_1_3_Highways`)

Contract from `docs/pypcg/stages/s08-brief.md`. Official graph: 34 nodes (1 parameter, 3 input, 2
generation, 5 dropped, 23 counted "python").

That "23" is a count of top-level nodes, and it is the wrong unit for this stage. Every "python" node
here is a `PCGPrimitives` wrapper, and their internals are part of the port surface (the task's own
instruction: *"the contract's python 23 counts each primitive subgraph as one node; their computation
is yours too"*). The real sizes, read out of `Saved/graph_dumps/` on 2026-10-17:

| primitive | wrapper | its own computation | total |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Transform_Resample` | 14 | `Transform_Resample_CoreProcess` 15 | 29 |
| `Transform_Project` | 21 | `Transform_Project_CoreProcess` 129 | 150 |
| `Transform_Areas_Expand` | 12 | `Transform_Areas_Expand_CoreProcess` 8 | 20 |
| `Filter_Tags` | 12 | `Filter_Tags_FilterInputTags` 11 | 23 |
| `Assign_ShapeGrammarDefinition` | 14 | CoreProcess 48 + innerLoop 29 + PrepareOutputs 11 | 102 |

plus `Get_ActorDataByRef` (4), `Get_MeshTerrain`, `Get_SplineControlPoints`, `CreateSpline`,
`MakeConcrete`, `Projection` and the rest of the graph's own nodes. The honest surface is **~330
nodes, not 23** -- stage 7 found the same ~14x factor on its own count.

## 2. The published output is a native chain, not a Python output

The contract's output tag sits on the **end** of a chain the port keeps:

```
Assign_HighwayShapeGrammar.Footprints        (native, kept)
  -> Subgraph_0 Transform_Areas_Expand 1500  (native, kept)
    -> AddTag_Highway "Highway"              (native, kept)
      -> output node
```

Measured on the live demo level (2026-10-17): the stage-8 volume's tagged output is exactly **two
`PCGPolygon2DData`**, both tagged `Highway`, `Primitive:TransformAreasExpand`, `Type:Polygon2D`, with
areas 433 468.16 m2 and 393 501.17 m2, plus 81 629 spawned instances over 10 meshes.

The Python node's own pin is therefore a **spline**, not a polygon: it does the read side up to and
including `AttributeMathsOp_3`, and hands the level spline to the kept grammar node. That is what the
official graph does too -- `AttributeMathsOp_3`'s `Out` is `Assign_HighwayShapeGrammar`'s
`PrimaryInput`.

## 3. The computation, as numbered steps

Each step names the official nodes it replaces; where the work is inside a primitive, the primitive
and its own nodes.

1. **Read the two highway splines** -- `Subgraph_7 Get_ActorDataByRef "Get Highway Spline Actors"`,
   a second concrete `Get_ActorDataByRef`, over `HighwaySpline2` and `HighwaySpline3`. Measured: two
   OPEN curves with 12 and 11 control points, 8 652.68 m and 7 883.71 m long, and every control point
   at Z 5550. Their dense polylines are 1 223 and 940 points.
2. **Flatten** -- `AttributeMathsOp_0 "Flatten Spline to Z = 0"`: `$Position.Z <- 0` at metadata
   domain 0. The dense polyline comes along in the port: the resample walks the CURVE, and highway 2
   is 8 652.68 m as a curve against 8 494.84 m through its 12 control points.
3. **Resample at 80 m** -- `Subgraph_9 Transform_Resample`, whose overrides are `Distance` 8000 and
   `SamplingMode` Distance. **The node's TITLE says "Resample Spline (40m intervals)" and the override
   says 80 m; the override is what runs.** Its `InputsProcessingGraph` is
   `InputsProcessing_ForceSpline`, and its CoreProcess (`Transform_Resample_CoreProcess`, 15 nodes) is
   `SplineSampler_1` in Distance mode with `DistanceIncrement` = 8000 and `bTreatSplineAsPolyline`
   off, then `CreateSpline_0` (open) and `CreateSpline_1` (closed) and the two `AddTags` that write
   `Type:Spline` / `Type:ClosedSpline`. Measured against the engine's own
   `get_location_at_distance_along_spline` at 8000 cm: **110 points for highway 2 and 100 for highway
   3**, and the port returns exactly those counts on the recorded inputs.
4. **Project onto the terrain** -- `Subgraph_8 Transform_Project`, `SecondaryInput` =
   `Subgraph_3 Get_MeshTerrain "Get Final Mesh Terrain"` with `MeshPartitionQueryType` Final and
   `MeshPartitionBoundSize` 300 000 cm. Its CoreProcess is 129 nodes: a `SplineSampler`, a
   `SurfaceSampler` over a `CreateSurfaceFromPolygon2D`, a nearest-point projection, and
   `AttributeMathsOp_0/1` lerping `$Position.Z` by `ProjectMask`. The port reads the same surface
   through `pypcg.ue.terrain.Terrain.height` -- the data's own `k2_sample_point`, the same ray the
   `PCGMeshPartitionData` answers -- and leaves a point the surface does not answer for at its own Z,
   which is what `ProjectMask` does at a miss. **Measured: the terrain under both highways is flat at
   Z -600 cm in the city, and 53 of highway 2's 110 samples fall outside the mesh partition and keep
   the flatten step's Z 0.**
5. **Clamp, rebuild, level** -- `GetSplineControlPoints_0` reads the projected spline's control
   points; `AttributeMathsOp_1` clamps `$Position.Z` to [-500, 1e9] (the two numbers are the node's
   inline pin defaults, `DefaultValue2 = -500` and `DefaultValue3 = 1000000000`, recorded as
   `pin_defaults` `[1]` and `[2]`); `CreateSpline_0` builds a LINEAR spline from them; `AttributeMathsOp_2`
   sets `$Rotation.Roll` to 0 and `AttributeMathsOp_3` sets `$Rotation.Pitch` to 0.
6. **The footprint** (native) -- `Assign_HighwayShapeGrammar`, an `Assign_ShapeGrammarDefinition`
   whose `ShapeGrammarDefinition` override is
   `SGD_ElevatedHighway_oneWay` and whose `SpawnAssets` is false. The primitive's `Size` pin is
   `GetParam_HighwayWidth`, i.e. the `Highway_Width` parameter. Its CoreProcess (48 nodes) reads the
   data asset's `orientation` property (`GetPropertyFromObjectPath_0` -> `Branch_0`): for a `Spline`
   orientation it takes the open branch, and `AddTags_1` unions `CreatePolygon2D_1`
   (`ForceOpen`, `EndType` Butt, `OpenPolygonWidth` = Size) with `OffsetSpline_0` and
   `Polygon2DOperation_1`, tagging the result `TEMP_Footprint`. `PrepareOutputs` (11 nodes) then
   splits `TEMP_Footprint` items into the `Footprints` pin (Polygon2D) and the `Points` pin, stamps
   `Primitive:AssignShapeGrammarDefinition`, and drops the temporary tag.
7. **Grow by 15 m** (native) -- `Subgraph_0 Transform_Areas_Expand`, override `Expand` 1500. Its
   CoreProcess is 8 nodes: `FilterDataByType_0` keeps Polygon2D, `OffsetPolygon2D_0` offsets by
   `Expand`, `Branch_1` splits on `UnionBeforeOutputting` (false in this graph, so `Output A`), and
   `Polygon2DOperation_0 Union` is wired to `Output B` and therefore never runs. `PrepareOutputs`
   stamps `Primitive:TransformAreasExpand`.
8. **Tag** (native) -- `AddTag_Highway` writes `Highway`, which is what stages 9, 13 and 18 filter on.

Steps 6-8 stay native because the contract names 6 (`Assign_HighwayShapeGrammar`) and 7
(`Subgraph_4`, which hangs off the pillar chain) as the generation to keep, and 7's register and 8 are
two one-node wrappers around engine elements with nothing to port.

## 4. What the width actually is, measured four ways

This was the largest time sink of the task, and the resolution is a pair of conventions plus one
composition, all measured rather than derived.

**The two primitives' offset arguments are different things.** `CreatePolygon2D`'s
`OpenPolygonWidth` is the band's FULL width: `PCGCreatePolygon2D.cpp:256` passes it to
`UE::Geometry::PolygonsOffset`, and a 36 118.85 cm straight path at 1900 gives a band 1900 cm across
(`+-950` about the path), 6 862.58 m2. `pypcg.ue.ops.Ops.path_offset(path, d)` makes a band of
HALF-width `d`: a 100 000 cm straight path at `d` = 500 / 1000 / 2000 / 2500 comes back spanning y =
500 / 1000 / 2000 / 2500 with area `100000 x 2d`, and on the recorded highway spline at its own
mid-path perpendicular the half-width is exactly `d` (2000 -> 1002, 2500 -> 1252.5, 5000 -> 2505).
**So a port of a `CreatePolygon2D` calls `path_offset(path, W / 2)`,** and the port's `ribbon` (removed
from the final module because the grammar node is native) had to divide by two. Stage 7's
`pypcg.geo.roads.outline` passes `width` unchanged, which its own measured 36 118.85 x 1900 band
confirms is right for `path_offset`'s convention only if `Transform_Outline`'s `Width` is twice its
offset argument -- a question outside this task's scope, recorded here so the next reader checks it.

**What the stage actually publishes.** Generating the OFFICIAL graph at three widths and reading the
published polygon:

| `Highway_Width` | official area (2 polygons) | official half-width, mid-path perpendicular |
|---|---|---|
| 2000 | 433 468.16 / 393 501.17 m2 | 2500.01 cm |
| 4000 | 606 862.23 / 550 901.63 m2 | -- |
| 8000 | 953 641.98 / 865 702.57 m2 | -- |

**And what reproduces it.** Re-deriving the two polygons from the recorded slim splines, three
compositions, each offset then `expand(1500)`:

| construction | areas (m2) | implied half-width, `area / perimeter` |
|---|---|---|
| `path_offset(W / 2 = 1000)` + 1500 | 316 447.97 / 347 111.50 | 1 990 / 1 991 |
| **`path_offset(W = 2000)` + 1500** | **395 565.56 / 433 894.75** | **2 485 / 2 486** |
| `path_offset(W / 2 + 1500 = 2500)` + 1500 | 435 124.35 / 477 286.38 | 2 731 / 2 733 |
| official | 393 476.32 / 433 468.31 | 2 485 / 2 486 |

The middle row is the one that matches: **+0.10 % and +0.53 % on area, and the same implied width to
1 cm.** So the published polygon is what `path_offset(W)` plus `Transform_Areas_Expand(1500)` makes,
and the published half-width is `2500.01 cm = W / 2 + 1500` at `W` = 2000, i.e. `(W + 3000) / 2`. The
area is linear in the parameter with slope `86.697 m2 per cm`, i.e. `(W + 3000) x L`.

Why the two statements are one: `path_offset`'s HALF-width at distance `W` is W's full width when
`W` is read as `CreatePolygon2D`'s argument, and `Assign_ShapeGrammarDefinition`'s inner loop passes
`Size` to `CreatePolygon2D`'s `OpenPolygonWidth` after `AttributeMathsOp_0` multiplies it by 0.5
(`AttributeMathsOp_1` in the non-loop core multiplies by 0.5 and -0.5 for the two closed offsets).
So the grammar node's own band is `W / 2` half-width, and re-deriving it needs `path_offset(W / 2)` --
which the first row shows does NOT match. The composition that matches is the **whole stage**,
grammar node included, which is why the middle row is the right control for V1: it is a re-derivation
of the published polygon, not of the grammar node's internal band. The grammar node's own internals
are left at their measured behaviour and not chased further, because they are native and the published
output already reproduces to 0.1 %.

The V1 check reads the published width back as `area / perimeter`: for the official polygons that is
2 485 / 2 486 cm, and the 2 500 cm the formula `W / 2 + 1500` predicts is 0.6 % away, which is the
polygon's own 2 442-vertex discretisation.

## 5. `Highway_Width`'s demo override is NOT an empty override

The generated contract prints `""` for `Highway_Width`'s demo override. `port_brief.py`'s table writes
`json.dumps(demo.get(name, ""))`, so `""` there means **the demo has no override for this parameter at
all** -- `Tools/pypcg/data/demo_overrides.json` carries only `Partitioned` and `PartitionGridSize` for
this stage. The volume runs on the graph's own 2000.

That matters because of what an empty override would do. Measured on the live instance
(`pyg_probe_t11_full.py`): `set_instance_parameter(inst, "Highway_Width", '{"v": ""}')` returns **no
error** and reads back as **0**. So an empty string on a Double parameter is silently 0, not "leave it
alone", and a stage whose default was copied from the empty string would build every footprint 3000 cm
too narrow -- half the official area, which is exactly the failure the brief's diagnosis order warns
about. The slim graph ships `Param(2000.0, ...)`, `phase_gen` reports `overrides.skipped` =
`{PartitionGridSize}` only on every slim run, and `Highway_Width` is never in the override bag, so it
takes the declared default rather than a copied empty value.

## 6. Deliberate differences, and what could not be reproduced

1. **The resample distance is the override's 80 m, not the brief's 40 m.** The brief's "resample at
   40 m" is the node TITLE; `Subgraph_9`'s `Distance` override is 8000. The brief also cannot know
   this, and the title is wrong. The port uses 8000 and its sample counts match the engine's exactly.
2. **`Subgraph_6 "Filter: Road-Modified Terrain"` is not kept.** It filters the Roads actor output for
   `ModifiedTerrain` and has no outgoing edge in the official graph -- nothing reads it.
3. **The pillar chain stays native.** `AttributeCast_1` (Asset -> AssetString), `AttributeFilter_1`
   (`AssetString` `Substring` "Round"), the three `FilterByType` nodes, `Projection_0` onto
   `MakeConcrete_1` of the RoadFootprints polygon, `AttributeFilter_2` (`$Density` `Equal` 1),
   `Reroute_0/1` and `Branch_0` all stay, fed the kept grammar node's `Points`. Porting them means
   reproducing `PCGProjectionSettings` against a concrete surface, and the contract keeps the
   generation half.
4. **One input pin for the two spline actors, not two.** The official `Subgraph_7` reads BOTH actors in
   ONE node. The first slim build wired that one node into two separate Python pins and the stage
   published **four** footprints for two highways (measured: 433 873 / 395 551 / 433 873 / 395 551),
   because a PCG node's output is delivered to each consumer pin whole. One pin, `HighwaySplines`,
   carries both. **This is a new trap for every later stage that reads N actors through one node into
   N Python pins.**
5. **The port's published polygon is not vertex-identical to the official's.** The port's comes out of
   `PCGCreatePolygon2D`/`PolygonsOffset`/`PCGOffsetPolygon2D` discretised at the engine's own
   tolerance and carries 205 and 225 vertices against the official 2 442 and 1 730. The areas agree to
   +0.09 % and +0.52 % and the bbox to 3 640 cm on the worst edge, so this is a discretisation
   difference, not a geometry one.

### 7.3 V1's limits, recorded rather than hidden

`run_stage` reports `replayed: 0`, and that is a real gap rather than a clean bill. `s08.json` records
its inputs, but the `MeshTerrain` input is a `PCGMeshPartitionData` and `geo.to_json` gives it
`{"kind": "Terrain"}`, which `from_json` refuses; `run_stage` therefore falls into its `inputs = None`
branch and does not replay. **So V1's determinism claim is really "the five runs agree", not "a replay
reproduces them".** The module's own repeatability is pinned offline instead
(`test_the_stage_is_deterministic_across_repeats`), and the terrain-dependence is what makes a replay
impossible without re-reading the level. The same limitation applies to any stage with a surface input.

## 8. What the V1 check can and cannot see

`pypcg/checks/s08.py` reads whichever kind the volume published:

- the **recorded run** holds the level SPLINES (the Python node's pin), so it checks the spline is
  linear, that every Z is inside the clamp band, and that the projection ran at all (a spline whose
  Z is 0 everywhere never left the flatten step);
- the **live volume** holds `PCGPolygon2DData`, so it checks the polygon is well formed and that
  `area / perimeter` -- the half-width a long thin ribbon implies -- is `Highway_Width / 2 + 15 m`
  within 600 cm. The official polygons read back 2 486 cm against the prediction's 2 500, so the
  tolerance is 0.6 % of the official's own discretisation error;
- the number of `Highway` items must equal the number of highway splines that came in, which is the
  check that catches the four-footprints bug of section 6.4.

The **oracle** half runs only when both sides hold polygons, which the recorded run does not, so it is
normally empty. The area and bbox evidence of section 7.2 therefore comes from the probe
(`pyg_probe_t11_oracle2.py`) rather than from V1's own oracle. That is the honest position: V1 pins the
port's own invariants, the V2 run pins the official comparison.
