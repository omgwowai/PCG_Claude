## 2. The computation

Every polygon boolean is `pypcg.ue.ops` (Clipper2 through GeometryScript, the engine's own back end);
`geo.areas` gives the engine's one-subject-at-a-time semantics.

### 2.1 `s16_park_geometry` (before the grammars, the building, the lake mesh and the lake writer)

1. **The park centre** -- `Subgraph_0 Place_Point_AtCenter` (`Place_Point_AtCenter_CoreProcess_Loop`) on the
   footprint: the box centre of its control points (60 150, 57 100, 0); its `$Rotation` is the ROTATOR of the
   polygon's point at the start of its first longest outer segment (`@Data.$LongestOuterSegmentIndex`; the
   CreatePolygon2D frame times the segment's `MakeFromXZ`, `geo.frames.vertex_points`), roll 0, turned back
   into a quaternion by `FRotator::Quaternion`. Two things had to be exact (section 4.1): the tie between the
   rectangle's two long edges goes to the FIRST (the strict rule on Python's bits picked edge 2 and turned the
   park 180 degrees), and the quaternion is the ENGINE's (`ops.rotator_quat`: SVML sincos; Python's sin / cos
   is one ulp off). Then the centre, the four lines and the Met are bit for bit.
2. **The four transverse lines** -- `Road_65th/79th/86th/97th_Line` (`Create_Spline_line`, `AtWorldOrigin`
   false, `Length` 20 500, `Position` X -38 000 / -10 000 / 4 000 / 26 000, yaw 87 / 94 / 88 / 93): the
   CoreProcess makes the two points (-L/2, 0, 0) and (+L/2, 0, 0), composes them with (`Position`,
   `Rotation`) (`CopyPoints_1`), then with the centre point (`CopyPoints_2`: `FTransform` target x source --
   rotate by the centre's quaternion, then translate), and builds a linear open spline. Measured: the four
   tapped lines (t02-t05).
3. **The road-centre outlines** -- `Subgraph_8 Transform_Outline` (`Width` 1) over the four lines and the
   footprint (`NamedRerouteUsage_20`): each open line becomes its 1 cm `ForceOpen` band (`geo.roads.outline`,
   the FULL width), the closed footprint its 1 cm annulus (`geo.roads.outline_ring`). Five polygons, lines
   first (t06).
4. **The border-road segments** -- `Extract_Footprint_Segments` (`Extract_Segments`, `CutAngleThreshold` 0,
   the primitive's default): with a zero threshold EVERY control point cuts, so each polygon gives one
   two-point segment per edge of its control points as `GetSplineControlPoints` lists them -- the outer ring,
   then each hole, as ONE cyclic sequence (the annulus gives 8 edges, two of them the jumps between its outer
   and its hole), degenerate edges included. 28 segments officially (t07) -> the kept `Spawn_Border_Road`
   (`SGD_Road_City`, `Size` 1600).
5. **The park pieces** -- `Subgraph_3 Compose_Areas_Exclusion`: the footprint less the five outlines -> 5
   pieces (47 995.6 / 43 995.8 / 27 996.6 / 55 995.2 / 23 996.8 m2, t08).
6. **The sidewalk ring** -- `Subgraph_13 Transform_Areas_Contract` (`Contract` 800; `UnionBeforeOutputting`
   is the primitive's default, FALSE) in each piece's own frame (`geo.areas.in_frame`, trap 64) -> the kept
   `Spawn_Intersections` (`SGD_Sidewalk_Corners`); `Subgraph_9 Extract_Segments` (threshold 0: one segment per
   edge, 20) -> the kept `Subgraph_5` (`SGD_Sidewalk_A`).
7. **The walkpaths** -- `AttributeMathsOp_0` sets the drawn splines' control points to z 0 (tangents kept);
   `Subgraph_20 Compose_Spline_Cut` (`DifferenceOrIntersection` true = `ClipPaths(Intersection)`) against the
   contracted pieces: the spline's polyline (`ConvertSplineToPolyLine`, error 1 cm -- the polyline `convert`
   already reads) is clipped, and each kept run becomes `SplitSpline(StartKey, EndKey)` of the curved spline
   (`PCGSplitSplines.cpp`: the split points at the keys nearest the run's ends, their tangents scaled by the
   key ratio, the control points between kept) -> 10 pieces, each spline's pieces LAST FIRST (t12) -> the
   kept `Subgraph_19` (`SGD_WalkpathSplineMesh`, `Size` 300).
8. **The vegetation areas** -- `Subgraph_17 Transform_Outline` (`Width` 800) on the flattened drawn
   splines' polylines -> 2 bands (8 685.96 / 9 076.36 m2); `Subgraph_18 Compose_Areas_Exclusion`: the park
   pieces (step 5, NOT the contracted ones) less the bands -> 15 areas (t14) -> `s16_central_park_visuals`.
9. **The lakes** -- `AttributeMathsOp_1` z 0; `Offset_Lake_Z` (+20, `Transform_Offset` per point: on a
   spline that is `TransformPoints` on its control points, then `OffsetSpline` -- the position moved by the
   offset rotated by the control point's own rotation, `MakeFromXZ(tangent, up)`, the tangents scaled by
   each segment's new/old chord length); `Subgraph_16 Transform_Splines_Reverse` (the control points in
   reverse order, every tangent negated, `CurveCustomTangent`) -> the kept `Subgraph_14`
   (`SGD_Sidewalk_CITY_EDGE`, `Size` 850).
   `Subgraph_44 Transform_Project` (the control points onto the Final terrain by a vertical ray; the spline
   comes back with AUTO tangents, `(prev->p + p->next) / 2`, bit for bit on all 38 tapped points) and
   `Subgraph_45 Transform_Level` (Min: every control point at the lowest; the tangents are the projected
   ones, not recomputed) -> the kept `Lake_Mesh` (`MI_ocean`). `Subgraph_56 Transform_Offset` (-50 cm, the
   same per-point rule: each control point moved by `RotateVector(MakeFromXZ(tangent, up), (0, 0, -50))`,
   which tilts with the tangent's slope -- XY moves up to 0.7 cm; 37 of 38 tapped points bit for bit, the
   last 1.5e-11 cm) -> the lake bed, AUTO tangents (bit for bit) -> `Subgraph_55` (native, section 3.3) ->
   the kept `Subgraph_53` Write_MeshTerrain at sub-priority 6.
10. **The Met** -- `Met_Point` (`Create_Point`, `Location` (875, 1 750, 50), copied onto the park centre);
    `Met_Footprint_Shape` (`Create_Spline_Shape`, Ngon 4, `Width` 8 000 x `Length` 3 750, `Position`
    (-5 000, 5 625, 50), on the Met point: stage 5's rectangle, corner order (-1, 1), (1, 1), (1, -1),
    (-1, -1)); `Subgraph_2` / `Subgraph_4` project and level (Min) its control points (z 1 433.2046 on the
    demo); `FilterDataByType_0` / `GetSplineControlPoints_0` / `CreateSpline_0` -> a closed linear spline ->
    the kept `Spawn_Met_Building` (CHA, 20-25 m, `Foundation`). The projection hands the rectangle back as a
    CURVE spline with auto tangents, the level keeps them, and `GetSplineControlPoints_0` writes each control
    point's `MakeFromXZ(tangent, up)` rotation (pitch +-0.9 / +-1.4 deg), which `CreateSpline_0` carries into
    the building -- and the building READS it (section 4.2). `Expand_Met_Footprint` (+1 000) on the
    UNPROJECTED shape (z 100) -> one exclusion polygon (5 750 m2).
11. **The exclusions** -- `AttributeMathsOp_2` (forest exclusion z 0), the flattened lakes, the expanded
    Met: as polygons (a closed spline's polyline, `Compose_Shared_InputsProcessing`), handed to
    `s16_central_park_visuals`; the flattened forest exclusion splines also go to the native exclusion-zone
    grass (section 3.2).

### 2.2 `s16_central_park_visuals` (after `Spawn_Border_Road` and `Spawn_Met_Building`)

12. **The road footprints** -- `Expand_Border_Footprint` (`Transform_Areas_Expand`, `Expand` 800 -- not the
    425 its title says; `UnionBeforeOutputting` false) on `Spawn_Border_Road.Footprints` (24 polygons) ->
    `Gather_0` (with the disabled transverse branch's nothing).
13. **The forest areas** -- `Exclude_Roads_From_Areas`: the 15 areas less the union of the forest
    exclusion, the lakes, the road footprints and the Met -> 17 areas officially (u04). `Subgraph_35
    Write_Attribute` (`@Data.Test`, a data-domain attribute nothing reads) is dropped.
14. **The split** -- `Filter_5_Areas` (`Filter_RandomChoice`, Ratio 0.5, `RandomPerDataset`): one Bernoulli
    per area (`geo.keep_each`), Python's own draw (spec E2). Officially 11 selected / 6 unselected. Both
    sets go to the native grass (section 3.2).
15. **The trees** -- `Fill_Forest` (unselected; 0.05 /m2) and `Fill_Forest_2` (selected; 0.02 /m2):
    `Fill_Areas_Randomly` (`SamplingPointExtents` 50, `Uniformity` 0) = the surface sampler's non-legacy grid
    (`geo.ground.fill_random`: cell `sqrt(1e4 / ppsm)`, one point per cell jittered over the cell less 2 x 50
    cm), then its `TransformPoints_1` (`PositionRange` +-200 X/Y relative to the drawn rotation, `RotationRange`
    pitch/roll +-5, yaw +-180, uniform scale 1 +- 0.25) and `OutputPointsExtents` 50; `Noise_Forest_Density(_2)`
    (`Write_Noise`: `$Density` uniform in [0, 1]) and `Filter_By_Density(_2)` (`$Density` > 0.4, a float);
    `Subgraph_10` / `Subgraph_7` `Custom_Projector_with_rotation_fix` (`geo.ground.project_point`, the point's
    scale and rotation); `Branch_6/7` on `UseMegaplantsTrees` -> `Subgraph_33` / `Subgraph_38` (four Black
    Alder field trees, `ScaleOffset` 0.7, `RandomScaleRange` 0.2 / 0.15, `RandomRotationRange` yaw 180) or
    `Assign_Trees` / `Assign_Trees_2` (nine / ten Megaplants, scale 1 +- 0.15, rotation (2, 2, 180) on the
    second) through `geo.assets`; `AttributeMathsOp_3/4` roll and pitch 0. Officially 2 935 + 723 filled,
    1 754 + 444 kept, 2 198 trees. All draws are Python's (E2) -> the kept `Subgraph_6`.
16. **The Met rooftop drop** -- `Subgraph_54 Transform_Offset` (-10 cm) on `Spawn_Met_Building`'s rooftop
    spline -> the kept `Met_Rooftops` (fitting assets); `Met_Rooftop_Mesh` reads the rooftop directly.

## 3. Deliberate differences

1. **New random numbers** (spec E2): the area split, the tree scatter, its jitter, density noise and kit
   picks are Python's. The grass is native and draws from the component seed, as officially, but it is laid
   on Python's split.
2. **The grass stays native (Step F, by estimate: the ported nodes take under 1 s, section 5).** The three grass chains --
   `Subgraph_28/30/31/12/64` (sparse: 50 ppsm at extents 15 = a 30 cm grid), `Subgraph_32/34/36/11/63` (dense:
   100 ppsm at extents 10 = a 20 cm grid) and `Subgraph_37/39/40/15/65` (the exclusion zone) -- produce
   949 370 of the official 965 006 instances. In Python that is ~1.6 M grid cells through a point-in-polygon
   test, ~950 000 points through the projector's four terrain rays each (stage 13: 32 000 points in 6.9 s,
   so ~200 s) and a 950 000-point write (trap 57: ~11 s). Far over Step F's 60 s, so the 15 nodes run native
   between the Python split and `Subgraph_6`, wired exactly as officially (`Gather_1` becomes edges).
3. **The post-grammar point chain stays native (ruling T17a's precedent, stage 10).** `Subgraph_27/42/46/48`
   (the projectors on the corner + sidewalk, walkpath, lake-edge and road points) and the street-prop
   roll/pitch fix and transparent-vehicle duplicate (`AttributeCast_1`, `FilterDataByAttribute_1..5`,
   `AttributeMathsOp_5/6`, `FilterDataByType_2`, `AttributeStringOp_1/2`, `CopyAttributes_0`,
   `AttributeCast_0`, `Gather_2`) are the same chain stage 10 kept native; `Gather_3` becomes edges into
   `Subgraph_6`.
4. **`Subgraph_55` stays native.** It projects all 263 358 Intermediate-P5 terrain points onto the lake bed
   (a closed CURVED spline converted to a polygon at 1 cm error) and hands every one of them, with its
   `SourcePositions` attribute, to the writer: a bulk pass-through whose only computation is a
   point-in-curved-polygon test. Python computes the lake bed it projects onto (step 9).
5. **Dropped as dead or disabled**: the transverse branch (section 0), `Subgraph_61`, `Subgraph_24` and the
   debug nodes, `Subgraph_35` (`@Data.Test`), every `Branch_*` / `Loop_*` / `GridPartition` of the partition
   machinery, `AttributeRename_0`, `AttributeBooleanOp_0` (its pin stays empty, as officially).
6. **Python-written polygons and splines carry their own frame** (trap 68): the grass fills lay their grid in
   the polygon's frame, so their cell alignment -- and the native grass count -- follows Python's polygon,
   not the official park frame (60 150, 57 100, yaw -90). Recorded, not chased (ruling T22a, the wrap-up).

## 4. What had to be found, and what is not reproduced

1. **The centre's last bit (fixed).** Measured against the taps: with Python's `MakeFromXZ` quaternion the
   lines and the Met came out within 1.5e-11 cm, and the Met's CHA building differed by +-8 props per mesh
   (`AC_Window_A02` 22 -> 14, ...: trap 62). The rotator route above with the engine's quaternion
   (`pyg_probe_t22f_quat.py`: a one-ulp search found the tapped bits at yaw 34.22265945387145, which
   `vertex_points` gives) makes the centre, all four lines and the Met rectangle bit for bit
   (`pyg_probe_t22f_centre.py`). `unreal.Rotator(...)`'s constructor rounds to float32 (trap 74), so the ops
   build the rotator by attribute assignment.
2. **The Met building reads its footprint's control-point rotations (fixed).** With the footprint bit for bit
   but identity rotations, the walls matched and the props still differed; with the tapped rotations (step 10;
   offline within 1e-15 deg of t28) every one of the 46 `SM_BLDG_*` meshes (1 067 instances) equals the
   official. The writer keeps their doubles: the geometry node sets `EXACT_SPLINE_ROTATORS`
   (`convert._rotator_exact`; the default path is unchanged for every other stage). Trap 75.
3. **Last bits that do not show** (all instance counts equal): the lakes (+20 / -50 offsets run in the lake's
   yawed frame officially, in the world here: 1.5e-11 cm), the contracted pieces and the sidewalk segments
   (<= 4.4e-11 cm; Clipper's start vertex differs on 4 of 5 pieces), the border segments (the same 28, another
   order: the band polygons start elsewhere), the vegetation areas (the same 15, another order), the walkpaths
   (<= 0.0045 cm: `FindInputKeyClosestToWorldLocation` returns a FLOAT key, `geo.curves.nearest_key` a double).
4. **The grass on Python polygons** (section 3.6): the native fills lay their grid in the Python polygon's
   frame. Not separable from the split's own randomness (section 5).
